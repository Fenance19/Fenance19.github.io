3/2 Stochastic Volatility Model & Option Pricing

Extracted the implied-volatility skew from NVDA April ITM calls via Newton's method, then showed that transplanting a single-maturity IV curve onto a later expiry produces systematic Black–Scholes mispricing. Built a 3/2 stochastic-volatility pricer — variance driven by a superlinear γY3/2 diffusion, simulated with a correlated Euler–Maruyama scheme (100k paths × 62 steps, variance calibrated to Y0 = σ²ATM ≈ 0.156) — and benchmarked it against Black–Scholes across 162 June ITM contracts. Mapped the model's parameter sensitivity, isolating the regime where stock–vol correlation ρ actually bites: negligible at realistic vol-of-vol, but at high γ negative ρ steepens the pricing curve while positive ρ flattens it. Closed by swapping pseudo-random sampling for Owen-scrambled Sobol sequences to collapse Monte Carlo variance at fixed compute.

Prediction Market Factor Model — Kalshi

Asked whether information embedded in Kalshi macro prediction-market prices is a priced source of systematic equity risk. Built a monthly long-short factor (F_KAL) from the Kalshi Becker Dataset and embedded it in an augmented three-factor model alongside the Fama–French market factor and a Google Trends attention factor. Ran the full gauntlet — Fama–MacBeth cross-sectional regressions, a GRS joint-alpha test (F = 0.20, fails to reject), Newey–West HAC inference, expanding-window out-of-sample evaluation (marginal OOS R² vs CAPM = −0.20), and Benjamini–Hochberg correction — and showed a market-neutral F_KAL-sorted long/short earns a −0.88 Sharpe. The verdict was deliberately honest: a small, marginally significant cross-sectional premium that does not convert into robust out-of-sample predictability or a tradeable edge.

Prediction Markets: Priced Efficiently?

Built probability-weighted implied forecasts for CPI, GDP and unemployment directly from Polymarket contract prices, then ran a forecasting horse race against the Bloomberg analyst consensus, AR(1) / random-walk baselines, and a suite of ML learners (Elastic Net, PCR, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, MLPs). Enforced strict look-ahead controls and judged accuracy with Diebold–Mariano and Mincer–Zarnowitz tests. Prediction markets reacted fastest to new information and won at short horizons on the hard-to-herd releases, while consensus economists kept the edge on routine high-frequency prints.

Information-Based CAPM — Polymarket × Attention

Augmented the CAPM with two forward-looking, information-based factors: a Polymarket economic-expectation factor built from 140+ macro event contracts, and a Google Trends search-volume attention factor following Da, Engelberg & Gao (2011). Engineered the end-to-end pipeline — Polymarket Gamma API, pytrends, CRSP and the Ken French library — with stationarity testing and look-ahead lagging, then compared nested factor specifications against an XGBoost benchmark with feature-importance analysis.
